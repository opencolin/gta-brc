# The Temple, for the game (researched 2026-10-27)

The most recent Temple that was built is the 2026 **Temple of the Moon**. This is the reference for redrawing the game's Temple in 2D (`src/game/PlayaWin.tsx` drawTemple, `src/game/citymap.ts`) and rebuilding it in 3D (`src/game/three/places.ts`). No CC-licensed 3D model of it exists (see the end), so it is built in code from this description. Everything below was found and verified by two independent readers from the sources listed; the photo URLs are references only, nothing is copied.

## 2026: Temple of the Moon

**Artists:** James Gwertzman (lead artist and designer) with architect Annie Locke Scherer (parametric design and renderings), the Moonlight Collective and the Temple Build Crew. Project managers Erin O'Brien and Sabrina Miller, build lead Tim Priest, lighting lead Kama Wybieralska, in partnership with the Temple Builders Guild. Source: journal.burningman.org 2026/02 announcement and 2026temple.com/about-the-temple.

THIS IS THE MOST RECENT TEMPLE THAT WAS ACTUALLY BUILT. It stood at Black Rock City 2026 and was burned on Sunday 6 Sept 2026 (Yahoo/USA Today photo captions). The 2025 event is no longer the latest.

CONCEPT: a night-blooming desert cactus flower, the Queen of the Night (Epiphyllum oxypetalum). Radial, flower-like plan, 5-fold in the middle and 8-fold at the fence. (Journal Feb 6 2026; 2026temple.com/temple-design.)

PLAN, centre outward (counts from the Journal and the design page; layout confirmed by my reading of the aerial photos):
1. Central tower and chamber. The design page says the tower is 30 ft tall. Journal: five archways open into a cathedral-like central chamber with curved benches. The tower is crowned by hinged 'crown petals' that flare out like a tulip or lotus. They close by day for shade, open at sunset and close at sunrise. In the photos the crown is a ring of roughly 16 pointed, triangular timber petals; the count is my estimate, not an official figure. Seen from straight above, the middle of the crown is a closed, pleated disc that looks orange, pink and red. I could not find what that disc is made of.
2. The stamen is a tree-like vertical element rising through the centre. The Journal gives 52 ft at its highest point. The design page and the post-event Archipanic article say up to 50 ft. In the Dezeen photo it is a fan of about 6 to 8 slender, dark, curved stems, each tipped with a glowing warm-white globe. It glows at night as a beacon. The Dezeen article says the core's stamen ended in lights.
3. Five curved 'petal arms' leave the tower in a pinwheel and fold back on themselves. They are curved crescent walls with benches and five long alcoves for reflection and offerings. From above they read as five interlocking loops. (Design page: five arms folding back on themselves. Journal: five long alcoves.)
4. 20 free-standing outer petals sit in a ring between the arms and the fence. In the photos they are curved, pointed, half-shell or sail-like wedges lying on the playa. They are placed so there is no straight line to the Temple. Visitors wind between them in an indirect approach modelled on Richard Serra's corridors. Smaller petals also give shade and seating (Dezeen Sept 2026). 'Twenty' is stated on the design page and by Archipanic.
5. A perimeter circular fence has 8 gates at 45 degree spacing, one per moon phase. Each gate is a round, ring-shaped arch of faceted plywood made of 10 panels, each 28 in tall by 23 in wide by 1/2 in thick. The panels carry CNC-cut community artwork (at most 50 percent cut away) and are backlit by LEDs, so the cutouts glow at night (2026temple.com news, call for submissions). In the photos the fence is a low diagonal-lattice or cross-hatched slat fence, about chest height against a bicycle (my estimate), with globe lamps on posts at intervals.

SIZE: the sources conflict. The Journal (Feb 6 2026), Business Insider and Wikipedia say about 170 ft in diameter including the perimeter fence. The design page (Jan 2026) and Archipanic (Sept 2026) say the fence is 220 ft in diameter. Tower 30 ft. Stamen 50 to 52 ft. No source gives the height of the arms or petals. From the photos the arms look about 15 to 20 ft and the outer petals about 8 to 15 ft, but that is my estimate only.

MATERIAL AND CLADDING: timber and plywood only, partly reclaimed wood (2026temple.com news). Straight timber pieces form doubly-curved surfaces using parametric design (Grasshopper). Curved walls are articulated by 'closely spaced vertical elements' (Journal), seen as dense vertical slats. The crown and lattices are open timber ribs. The wood is unpainted and unstained, a pale honey or blond plywood colour in daylight (photos). Visitors hung offerings on the slatted walls (Dezeen).

NIGHT: in the Rand Larson dusk and night photos the whole Temple glows warm amber-gold from inside. The crown petals are lit, the stamen globes glow, the 8 gates glow as rings, and the fence lamps make a ring of dots. The silhouette is a low wide mound with a flared crown and a bouquet of lights, readable from far across the playa. A top-down plan photo shows the circle fence, 20 outer petals, 5-arm rosette and crown ring clearly.

One more point: the Temple was prebuilt in an Oakland warehouse and trucked to the playa (72 fence panels, 10 inner petals). The build crew arrived under the new moon, and the Temple was released by fire a few days before the next new moon (Journal).

## 2025: Temple of the Deep

**Artists:** Miguel Arraiz Garcia (lead artist; Spanish architect from Valencia, Burning Man Regional Contact for Spain-Valencia), with Spanish collaborators Javier Molinero, Arqueha (renderings) and Javier Bono, plus Temple Builders Guild members (Journal, 10 Jan 2025).

The built Temple of the 2025 event, burned 31 Aug 2025 (My Modern Met). It is the fallback now that the 2026 Temple exists.

SIZE: 44 ft high by 105 ft diameter (Journal 10 Jan 2025, design-stage figures; repeated by STIRworld, Wikipedia and My Modern Met). A horizontally oriented design, not a tall tower (Journal quoted by We Rave You).

PLAN AND FORM: the main element is a heptagon (7-sided plan) with radial symmetry (My Modern Met; Archipanic: 'beneath a heptagonal dome'). In the photos the exterior mass is a dark, faceted, truncated inverted frustum. It is wider at the top than at the bottom, like a black rock or upturned bowl hovering over a lower base, with a flat top and a central opening. The exterior is described as monolithic, rough-hewn and fractured (Dezeen). Seven narrow entrances stand for the seven stages of grief. They lead along paths that converge in a central agora (open meeting space) with amphitheatre-style tiered seating that mirrors the Black Rock City plan. Small alcoves or chapels with no altar surround it. A spiralling vortex of tubing circles the central opening for ventilation (Archipanic).

SURFACE AND COLOUR: crafted from wood. The skin is black, split into uneven polygonal pieces. The cracks glow gold or amber from within, a reference to kintsugi (Journal: 'fractured surface ... glows from within'). The lower level around the entrances and the colonnade reads warm timber colour and is lit orange in the photos. Low, zig-zag raised ramp or boardwalk-fence segments, lit warm orange from below, form an irregular ring around it (my reading of the Scott London photos).

NIGHT: a black faceted saucer or heptagon with a glowing crack network (thin gold lines on black), standing on a warm-lit base. It is very distinctive from the air.

No source I found gives separate heights for the base level and the top.

## 2024: Temple of Together

**Artists:** Caroline Ghosn (lead artist, playa name Glitter Kitty) with architect Maissa Sader of Kamai Studio (Journal 17 Jan 2024; Dezeen; Archipanic).

The built Temple of the 2024 event (25 Aug to 2 Sep 2024; Dezeen). It was lit at the end of the event.

SIZE: the structure including the fenced courtyard spans 94 ft in diameter and rises 70 ft above the playa (Journal). Archipanic gives the same figures in metres: 28 m diameter and 21 m high.

FORM: a stepped, bell-shaped or skirted pointed-arch mass. The Journal says the form honours the 'skirted bell shape' of David Best's Temple of Grace. Neo-Gothic pointed arches repeat throughout, with layered Art Deco symmetry. The main motif is two hands pressed in prayer, which form the archway at the 6:00 entrance. There is also a 3:00 entrance. In the aerial photos it is a symmetrical tiered mass climbing to a tall central spire. Smaller spires and arches sit around it, with arched wings curving forward and a fenced courtyard with lit posts across the front.

INTERIOR: a light-filled central chamber with communal seating, an altar that extends upward into a spire, rising lanterns, and multiple private alcoves.

CLADDING: reeded weaving, based on the wooden Khaizaran chair. Sustainable and repurposed wood, built by volunteers.

NIGHT: it glows warm gold and amber, with lantern-like lights picking out the arches and the spire. Visible in both photos listed.

I did not verify whether the fence is hexagonal (an Archpaper snippet says 'porous hexagonal ...' but the text was cut off).
